# 06 - UX, UI, and game juice

All new. Nothing from the sds UI layer carries over as code; a handful of tuned feel constants carry over as numbers.

## UX flows

- **Boot**: page load fades from a palette-matched color card straight into the field (one suspense boundary, all-cold load). Title floats over the live field with Play, the flock-size choice (25 / 75 / 200), and a quiet `Running as <name>` line with Edit. A friendly server-random name arrives in the background. Play is never disabled by identity or score-network state. Under five seconds from navigation to herding on a mid-range phone. No scene picker, no multi-stage loading choreography, no entrance product.
- **Solo**: Play drops the title away and gives you the dog. Client-local sim works offline. Finish shows time and personal best immediately. Online success adds a quiet rank and the leading times; online failure says the local time is safe and leaves every completion action usable.
- **Interruptions**: pause is instant and local. Losing the score service never pauses play, queues a modal or asks the player to retry.
- **Settings**: one small panel, and this is the whole of it: render quality (auto / high / medium / low), Reduce motion, Follow camera turning, colorblind-safe dog marker, a run-timer toggle, sound (mute everything, plus one slider per bus), keyboard remap, and a footer carrying the licence line with source and privacy links. Nothing else. Reduce motion is seeded from `prefers-reduced-motion` and the player's own choice wins from then on; the label is the platform string and the specificity sits in a helper line, "Steadier camera, softer effects.", which adds "Following your system setting." until they choose. Follow camera turning is off / gentle / quick, default gentle; while Reduce motion is on the rig is clamped DOWN to gentle and the row says so rather than disagreeing with the stored value. It clamps to the slowest profile that still tracks and deliberately not to off, because off pins the bearing and the movement basis tracks that bearing: pinning it also pins steering, so a held direction turns the dog through one corner and then runs it straight. Almost nobody chooses this setting - it follows the platform until the player touches the toggle, and phones carry it far more often than desktops - so off is a thing to be asked for, never a thing to be handed out. Its helper line gives the value's rate ceiling and not a time: a duration divided out of that ceiling ignores the dead zone and the lag ahead of it, so it is a floor rather than what the player waits, and the row is the accessibility row, where a number the setting cannot meet costs more than no number.

## UI principles

- React TSX only, in `app/src/ui/`. Subscribes to the same zustand store as the scene. Zero window globals, zero event-bridge glue, zero polling (the entire sds GameBridge pattern is forbidden).
- A design-token module (color, spacing, type scale, z-index, motion durations) is the ONLY styling authority, written before the first component. `useReducedMotion` respected from day one. Both were the only parts of sds's UI that converged correctly; start there.
- In-game HUD is nearly nothing: sheep-penned count (fills as a soft pictogram row or radial), optional timer (off by default; the zen default is no clock on screen), stamina as a subtle ring around or under the dog, not a bar in a corner. Every HUD element must justify existing against the Calm pillar.
- Touch-first layout: thumb-reachable controls, 44 px minimum targets, the same minimal HUD. Virtual stick + bark button on touch; keyboard/mouse and gamepad on desktop. Input produces one normalized intent shape consumed identically everywhere. The 44 px is a target, so the touch tap that names a sheep is floored at it; the stick's 48 px radius is a travel and not a target, and desktop hover is deliberately under it, because a hover leash wide enough to clear 44 px keeps a nameplate on an animal the cursor has left.
- Desktop precision: hold V (remappable) with movement to walk at 30% effort; release to run normally. Walk takes precedence over keyboard sprint while held. Shift remains sprint. The desktop control reminder shows both. Partial joystick movement retains its own analog effort. The 30% walk is unchanged by the movement conditioning below, and digital input bypasses the analogue response curve: a key is already a step command at one effort or the other, and putting 0.3 through the curve would deliver 0.16.
- Typography and menus feel like the game: painterly, warm, unhurried. No stock component library look.

## Carried feel constants (numbers, not code)

- Camera Follow mode: bearing lag tau 1.0 s, and the lag alone is not the contract - it runs under a rate ceiling and a dead zone (next bullet). Position lag tau 0.15 s, posK capped 0.3/frame, frame-rate-independent smoothing `1 - exp(-dt/tau)`. The aim is bolted to the bearing, centre plus forward times the look-ahead, so it contributes no rotation of its own and the look-ahead costs nothing; the look-ahead itself moves on the position lag, so a turning-law or orientation change is not a step in it. Rig height and aim are world-locked to a field datum of 0.06 m, the aim 1.6 m above it, so no term of the pitch is sampled from the ground and terrain passing under the dog cannot pitch the view. That is not the same as the pitch being constant: the pitch is `atan((eye height - datum - 1.6) / (radius + look-ahead))`, and all three of those terms move when the view profile or the turning law changes, each on the position lag. A phone rotated to portrait moves the pitch 1.66 degrees over 0.45 s, peaking at 12.3 deg/s; the widest first frame any profile change makes is 0.44 degrees, 27 deg/s. The ridge clamp is the only other hand on that axis and the only path by which ground reaches the height at all, so through ordinary play the pitch does hold. Ridge clamp: sample 7 points camera-to-dog plus one under the camera, clamp Y above max + 2.2 m clearance, snapping up and easing down on the position tau; at 14 to 17.5 m of elevation over 4.67 m of field relief it should effectively never engage.
- Follow bearing law, in this order: the target is the direction the dog is TRAVELLING, above 1 m/s, not the direction it faces, and the bearing holds below that; the dead zone comes off the error, with hysteresis at 0.75x the resting zone to keep turning the way it already is and 1.875x to reverse; an approach hold ramps the gain to zero between 100 and 120 degrees of error and releases after 20 m travelled inside the cone; then the lag; then the rate cap, integrated as cap times dt.
- Follow turning, the settings row: off = 0 deg/s, no dead zone, look-ahead 0, bearing armed on entry and on reseat only; gentle (default) = 25 deg/s, 20 degree dead zone, look-ahead x1; quick = 40 deg/s, 25 degree dead zone, look-ahead x1.25. Reduce motion clamps the rig down to gentle without writing to the stored value: the clamp can only ever lower the profile, so quick becomes gentle and a player who chose off keeps it.
- Follow geometry, by orientation: landscape 26 m back / 14 m up / 4 m look-ahead at a 45 degree vertical FOV; portrait 32.5 / 17.5 / 3 at 76 degrees; blended on aspect between 0.82 and 0.46. Classic position lag tau 0.325 s, the sds 0.05-per-frame-at-60 lerp converted once.
- Camera transitions: Classic <-> Follow 0.8 s, 1.2 s under Reduce motion, Studio 0.6 s, eased at both ends. Where an eye and an aim move together they move in polar form about the aim - bearing, horizontal radius, height - and not on independent Cartesian tracks. The straight line between two poses runs through the `lookAt` singularity, where the right vector collapses and the horizon flips in one frame, and a rate cap cannot make a path through a pole safe. That covers the mode swap, the Studio blend, the Studio framing's own ease toward a tab or preset, the re-armed Follow bearing and the exit from the completion phase. The completion pull-back itself is the one pair left on straight lines, and it is named here rather than left to be found: eye and aim run to their targets on one shared settle value, and it is made safe by BOUNDING the single term of its aim swing that closes on the eye rather than by re-shaping the path, because an orbit to a pose that points at the ground is still a picture that points at the ground. Anywhere else a Cartesian pair is a defect, not a tuning choice.
- Those durations are nominal. Each blend resolves its own weight against the rotation and the travel that weight would cause before committing it, so what ships is what the budget allows: 90 deg/s of view rotation for the WHOLE frame, scaled by 0.8 over the blend duration in force, so Reduce motion runs it at 60 rather than leaving the one player who asked for less motion with everyone else's peak, under the 55 m/s ceiling every camera position moves under. No stage bounds the picture on its own any more, and the Studio blend's separate 150 deg/s is gone: a second ceiling on a second stage is the defect described below, not a bound. The rates a stage does keep - the two recovery orbits, which shape a pay-back rather than bound a picture - are scaled by that same Reduce motion factor, so the setting slows every stage and not only the blend it is named after. A Studio entry between opposed bearings therefore takes several times the 0.6 s it nominally asks for, and that is the budget working rather than the duration failing. Frame delta capped at 0.1 s. The 90 deg/s figure is chosen rather than measured in motion, and is pending a motion review.
- A per-stage ceiling is not the contract, because stages COMPOSE. The Follow rig turns at its own 25 or 40 deg/s underneath a swap, a re-armed bearing is paid back on top of both as an orbit at 25 deg/s (a half-turn re-aim therefore unwinds over 6.5 s with the rig otherwise at rest, and 2.3 s when the rig is itself turning into the arc, at 30, 60 and 144 Hz alike), and the completion move and the Studio blend arrive on top of those. Independently capped rotations add, so the total on screen exceeds every individual ceiling, and a stage held back by a ceiling downstream leaves a backlog its own budget cannot see, which returns as a spike the frame that ceiling stops binding. What the inner ear responds to is the rotation of the final view direction, which exists in one place: the end of the frame, after every contributor. So the ceiling belongs to the composed view and is measured from the picture the camera was actually left at, never from a pose part-way down the frame that the camera never occupied. A composition over it means the rig is wrong and never that the bound should be raised - and a ceiling that bounds METRES bounds no rotation at all, because one shared factor marching a saturated eye and aim 33 m apart along their own straight lines turns the picture at twice 55 m/s over their separation. `tests/camera-composed-view.spec.ts` measures the composed view itself; STATUS.md carries what it reads today.
- The composed view comes no nearer than 30 degrees to straight down on any of those paths, and that floor is a degeneracy limit rather than a framing number: framing is art direction and is expected to move, degeneracy is not. The completion pull-back bounds the one term of its aim swing that closes on the eye, so the move never brings the view nearer the pole than the angle it started at, and never nearer than 45 degrees when it started further off than that. The second clause is the whole promise: a run that ends in Classic can enter the phase already inside it, at 43.1 degrees off straight down, and there the move holds the angle it found rather than lifting it, because the angle a run finishes on is the framing's and no clamp in the move can reach it. The mode swap's own polar blend cannot take the pitch outside 21.8 to 44.9 degrees at all, because both poses are decomposed about their own aims and the radius between them runs from 26 m to 40.71 m.
- Movement conditioning, applied to the intent between the devices and the sim so `sim/` and its trace fixtures are untouched: commanded speed ramps at 34 m/s^2 up and 45 down from a 3.5 m/s launch; the commanded direction turns at 65 m/s^2 of lateral acceleration divided by the speed the dog carries, floored at 40 deg/s and capped at 270, which is a 3.46 m turning circle at a full run and 9.62 m at a sprint, chosen against the sim's 8 m sheep flee radius so a run can correct inside the pressure zone and a sprint cannot; turn authority scales effort from full at 45 degrees off the dog's momentum to zero at 135, restored below 3 m/s; the commanded sprint ceiling eases to the walk ceiling across a stamina window derived from the sim's own sprint floor and drain rate, and the sim's ceiling is carried while the command is still above the walk speed. Input dt capped at 0.1 s.
- Camera basis for movement: sampled when the axis enters from neutral, re-sampled at 44 degrees of axis change and on a camera-mode change or a new sim, and between those samples it TRACKS the Follow bearing on a 0.2 s time constant. So a held direction curves with the camera instead of running off down the compass direction the key meant when it was pressed. Steering live off a camera that follows the dog is a feedback loop, and the basis used to be frozen for the whole hold to cut it; that was one brake too many once the bearing itself was rate-capped, and the ceiling on how fast a held direction can curve is now the Follow turning rate, because that is the only thing moving the bearing being chased. At the `off` end stop the camera does not turn, so the basis does not either and a held key stays world-locked. The one motion of the basis that is not the player's is the mode swap, which sweeps between framings for reasons the hand had no part in, so the sample is HELD for the length of that blend and the residue eased out afterwards. What is tracked is the rigs' own published bearing, interpolated as an angle between Classic's world forward and the Follow bearing on the eased weight the picture uses, and never the camera transform, which also carries the completion move and the Studio blend.
- Device shaping, one shared radial shaper: dead zone with rescale, then saturation, then the response curve. Touch stick radius 48 px with the origin re-anchored under the thumb past the rim, dead zone 0.06, saturation 0.92, and a 1 euro filter on the raw pixel offset (1.2 Hz at rest, beta 0.05). Gamepad dead zone 0.18, saturation 0.95. The curve is half linear, half cubic, so half travel is 0.3125 effort and the 30% walk band sits at the middle of the throw.
- Camera modes: Classic (top-down, world-axis WASD) and Follow (low cinematic). Classic input stays world-axis; camera-relative from above disorients. Free-orbit is a debug-only extra.
- Displayed animal turning, which is optic flow in the same way a camera turn is, on 200 bodies rather than one. Presentation-only heading smoothing, sim headings untouched: a lag plus a SUSTAINED ceiling in radians per second, so a 120 or 144 Hz display no longer turns an animal twice as fast as the 60 Hz it was tuned at. Sheep 0.1 s and 16.8 rad/s, dog 0.045 s and 8.4 rad/s, each of those rates being the per-frame step the animal shipped with read at 60 Hz. The per-frame step stays on as an anti-snap guard for a long frame, 0.28 rad on a sheep and 0.14 on the dog, because a rate times a long delta does not bound one.
- Grass interaction: oriented rounded-rect SDF in entity-local frame (never a world-axis ellipse), radius 1.02, strength 0.58; dog footprint halfLen 1.16 / halfWid 0.48 / falloff 0.68; sheep scale x1.25 / z1.45. Wind: three rotated noise octaves, variation 0.35-0.65 (one octave reads as a coherent wavefront; three average into flow).
- Bark config: `sim/` DEFAULT_BARK_CONFIG (spec/02), consumed identically by sim and presentation.

## Game juice (a first-class system, not garnish)

Owned by `app/src/scene/juice/` + audio hooks; driven by sim events and per-instance agitation state. Each item ships through the same critic loop as assets, judged in motion:

- **Sheep life**: gait bob with per-instance phase, ear flicks, tail wiggle, wool jiggle on direction change, startle ripple propagating through neighbors when the dog surges, head-down grazing when calm, tiny hop over the gate threshold.
- **Dog feel**: lean into turns, run cycle kicks up dust motes, sit + head tilt when idle 5 s, bark visualized as a soft expanding ring that bends grass.
- **Field response**: grass parting wakes behind every body, wildflowers spring back with overshoot, birds lift from the hero tree on the first bark.
- **Progress moments**: each penned sheep is a soft chime + a wisp of motion at the counter; the final sheep triggers the gate swinging closed, a slow camera pull-back, warm bloom lift, and the flock settling to graze. Completion is a sigh, not a firework. Under Reduce motion the gate and the bloom still run, shortened to 0.18 s, and the camera move does not run at all: the rig keeps tracking the dog and nothing is committed. A suppressed move that is still committed is not less motion but deferred motion - the picture freezes on the pose the phase was entered on while the dog runs on, and the gap it opens is paid off in one transition at the exit, which is how the setting came to make the commonest transition in the game worse than leaving it off.
- **Micro-transitions**: every UI element eases (tokened durations, 150-300 ms, reduced-motion honored). Nothing pops or teleports, in-world or in-UI.

## Forbidden

- Any second UI technology generation. If a component pattern changes, migrate all of it in one PR (sds ran three generations glued by 47 globals).
- HUD churn per frame through React state; HUD numbers read the store transiently.
- Exclamation marks, hype copy, or emoji in player-facing text. The voice is quiet and concrete.
- Dark-pattern retention anything: no streaks, no badges, no popups.
